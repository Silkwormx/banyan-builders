Measured, simplified, and made up

The jokes are the ride. These notes are the curb, so the story does not smuggle in a fake Mars.

Measured on Mars

  • Mars is a cold desert with a very thin atmosphere, almost all carbon dioxide. A NASA Goddard fact sheet lists about 95.1% carbon dioxide, about 0.16% oxygen, and a mean surface pressure of 6.36 millibars, well under one percent of Earth's air at sea level. Average temperature is about −59 °C. NASA Mars Fact Sheet
  • Sunlight arrives, but more weakly than at Earth. The same fact sheet lists solar irradiance of 586 watts per square meter, about 43% of Earth's. The air is too thin for liquid water to last in the open. Water is there as ice, including ice the Phoenix lander found in the ground. NASA Mars Facts
  • The red comes from iron oxide in the dust and rocks. By day a dusty sky looks yellow, brown, or butterscotch. Fine dust lets blue light pass a little more easily than red, then scatters that blue forward, so the blue stays near the Sun at sunrise and sunset. The rest of the sky does not turn blue. NASA: What sunrises and sunsets look like on Mars · Pathfinder sunset colors · Curiosity sunset at Gale crater
  • Phoenix found perchlorate, a few tenths of a percent, in all three soil samples its wet-chemistry lab tested. That is a salty oxidizer, not a bag of potting soil. Raw regolith is not used as a garden bed in this story. JPL: Phoenix results
  • A toaster-sized rover experiment called MOXIE made oxygen by splitting carbon dioxide. Its first run produced about 5 grams in an hour, roughly ten minutes of breathing for one person. Useful as a test. Not a breathable planet. NASA: first oxygen from Mars air · Perseverance science highlights
  • NASA does not expect living things to be thriving on Mars now. Nothing here is terraforming, and nothing here is a discovered Martian.

Simplified in this toy

  • The sun, tank, and sprout are on-off steps. They are not a sized life-support model, and the meters are not engineering data.
  • A real oxygen machine has to get very hot and still makes a small amount. The workshop kettle does not do that. It only shows the idea: power, then a filter, then a result you can see.
  • Dust really hangs in the air and can cover solar equipment. Brushing the panel clean for a flat fee is a story shortcut, not a dust-storm forecast.
  • Demo ANTS are a number in this browser. Spending them changes the picture. It does not buy anything outside the page.

Made up for the story

  • Talking ants, the prices, the blue door, the cinema night, and a sprout that "helps the air" are fiction. A sprout does not make this dome safe to open.
  • The clear ribs in the painting are story art. A habitat on Mars would have to be a sealed, pressurized, insulated, shielded shell. Ordinary glass does not hold in air, hold heat, or stop radiation by being transparent.
  • "Do more with less" is a builder's habit in the spirit of R. Buckminster Fuller. It is not a law of physics, and the dome is not a guaranteed utopia. Waste, dust, and maintenance remain.
  • The character sheet includes a cliff study with an ocean and a bell. That picture is not a place inside this dome, so the story shows only the dusk portraits from the top of the sheet.
